# Functional and Technical Framing of the AI Component — Pricing Control Tower

## 1. Context

Pricing Control Tower is a pricing governance application designed to analyze prices, promotions, business KPIs, business anomalies, price change requests, and pricing history.

The project already includes the following components:

* PostgreSQL database;
* FastAPI backend;
* Django web application;
* dbt pipeline;
* business KPIs;
* business anomaly detection;
* pricing workflow;
* RBAC;
* monitoring and CI/CD in progress.

The current phase focuses on framing the AI component before starting any development.

## 2. Objective of the AI Framing

The objective is to define an AI use case that is relevant, realistic, demonstrable, and aligned with the RNCP Bloc 2 requirements.

The framing must avoid developing an AI component that is too complex, poorly justified, or difficult to industrialize.

The selected approach prioritizes:

* simplicity;
* robustness;
* business value;
* demonstrability;
* traceability;
* compatibility with the remaining project timeline.

## 3. AI Use Cases Considered

Several AI use cases were analyzed.

| Use case                                       | Business value | Feasibility | Decision                 |
| ---------------------------------------------- | -------------: | ----------: | ------------------------ |
| Predictive pricing risk model                  |           High |      Medium | Not selected for the MVP |
| Automated price recommendation                 |           High |         Low | Rejected                 |
| Explanatory chatbot connected to business data |           High |        High | Selected                 |
| Agentic AI for data enrichment                 |           High |        High | Selected as a POC        |
| Weather-based decision support                 |         Medium |      Medium | Excluded from the MVP    |

## 4. Selected AI Approach

The AI component of Pricing Control Tower will be structured around two complementary parts.

### 4.1 Part 1 — Explanatory Chatbot Integrated into the Application

The first part is a business chatbot integrated into Pricing Control Tower.

Its role is to help users understand data, KPIs, anomalies, and business rules.

The chatbot will be connected to the application through controlled endpoints. It will remain read-only.

It will be able to:

* query application data;
* explain KPIs;
* interpret results;
* explain business rules;
* explain roles and permissions;
* help users understand detected anomalies;
* provide answers in natural language.

It will not be allowed to:

* modify a price;
* create a price change request;
* approve a request;
* reject a request;
* apply a price change;
* generate free SQL;
* write directly to the database.

### 4.2 Part 2 — Agentic AI POC for Data Enrichment and Data Generation

The second part is an agentic AI POC, initially developed separately from the main project.

Its goal is to take product data sources as input, use AI to enrich, structure, validate, and export clean data.

Possible input sources include:

* scraped data;
* CSV files;
* JSON files;
* other structured sources.

The POC will later be integrated into Pricing Control Tower to improve the generation of realistic fictional data.

The final objective is to provide more coherent data for:

* products;
* product families;
* prices;
* promotions;
* sales;
* business anomalies;
* demo scenarios.

## 5. Business Justification

### Explanatory Chatbot

The chatbot provides value by helping business users access relevant information faster.

It answers questions such as:

* “What is the revenue for France between two dates?”
* “List the price changes made for store X between two dates.”
* “List the products whose store price is not aligned with the country price for store X.”
* “Why is this KPI important?”
* “Which role can approve a price change request?”

It supports analysis without replacing human decision-making.

### AI Data POC

The POC addresses another need: improving the quality of fictional data.

Today, data is generated by a standard Python script. It works, but the generated data may lack business realism.

AI will help enrich product data and generate more credible scenarios for the project demonstration.

## 6. Technical Feasibility

Both AI parts are feasible with the existing architecture.

The chatbot will rely on:

* Django for the user interface;
* FastAPI for API endpoints;
* PostgreSQL for data storage;
* existing or new business endpoints;
* a separate AI service;
* controlled tools called by the agent.

The POC will rely on:

* CSV/JSON files;
* existing scraped product data;
* Python scripts;
* Pydantic or equivalent validation;
* CSV/JSON export;
* future integration through a PostgreSQL import script.

## 8. Target Dataset

### 8.1 Chatbot Dataset

The chatbot does not require a traditional supervised ML training dataset.

The target dataset is instead a reference and test dataset composed of:

* typical user questions;
* expected intents;
* expected parameters;
* expected business tool;
* expected answer;
* error cases;
* out-of-scope cases.

Example:

| Question                                                              | Intent                              | Expected tool                       |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| What is the revenue for France between two dates?                     | get_country_revenue                 | get_country_revenue                 |
| List the price changes for the Lille store                            | list_store_price_changes            | list_store_price_changes            |
| Which products have a store price not aligned with the country price? | list_store_country_price_mismatches | list_store_country_price_mismatches |

### 8.2 AI Data POC Dataset

The target dataset for the POC will be composed of products from multiple sources.

Possible inputs:

* raw product name;
* raw category;
* image URL;
* raw description if available;
* source;
* price if available;
* scraped product attributes.

Expected output:

* normalized product name;
* suggested product family;
* short description;
* business tags;
* seasonality profile;
* promotion sensitivity;
* confidence indicator;
* validation status.

## 9. Target

### 9.1 Chatbot

There is no supervised ML target in the traditional sense.

The functional target is to:

* correctly detect the user intent;
* correctly extract parameters;
* call the correct business tool;
* produce an answer that is faithful to the returned data.

### 9.2 AI Data POC

The POC does not aim to train a predictive model in the MVP.

The target corresponds to the expected enriched fields:

* `normalized_product_name`;
* `product_family`;
* `description`;
* `tags`;
* `seasonality_profile`;
* `promo_sensitivity`;
* `validation_status`.

If automatic classification is added later, the main target could be:

* `product_family`.

## 16. Role of the Chatbot in the Global AI Architecture

The chatbot is the main AI component integrated into the application.

Its role is to:

* query data;
* explain KPIs;
* help users understand results;
* interpret business anomalies;
* explain business rules, roles, and permissions;
* provide clear natural-language answers.

It is not responsible for data generation.

Data generation and enrichment are handled by the AI Data POC, then later by the integrated pipeline after validation.

## 18. Final Decision

The selected AI use case is not a traditional predictive pricing model.

The AI scope is structured around two parts:

1. **Pricing Data Assistant Agent**
   An explanatory chatbot integrated into the application, connected to business data, able to interpret results and business rules in read-only mode.

2. **AI-Assisted Product Data Enrichment POC**
   A separate agentic AI POC designed to enrich, validate, and prepare product data from multiple sources before future integration into Pricing Control Tower to improve fictional data generation.

This approach is selected because it is:

* realistic;
* demonstrable;
* aligned with RNCP Bloc 2 requirements;
* useful for the application;
* useful for data quality;
* compatible with an MLOps approach;
* secure by design.
