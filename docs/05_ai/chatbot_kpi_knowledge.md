# KPI Knowledge for Pricing Users

## Revenue

Revenue measures the total sales amount generated over a period.

Formula: `revenue = sum(quantity × paid_price)`

Business interpretation: Revenue reflects commercial performance but must not be read in isolation. High revenue can hide low margin if discounts are too aggressive. Always cross-check revenue with margin and volume before drawing conclusions.

Decision support: If revenue increases during a promotion, verify that margin did not deteriorate and that volume uplift is real before concluding that the promotion is successful.

## Margin

Margin measures the difference between revenue and the cost of goods sold.

Formula: `margin = revenue − cost`

Business interpretation: Margin is the profitability indicator. A promotion that boosts revenue but erodes margin below an acceptable threshold should be reviewed or stopped.

Decision support: Before approving a price change or extending a promotion, check the expected margin impact. A price reduction that brings margin below the business floor is not viable.

## Volume

Volume measures the number of units sold over a period.

Formula: `volume = sum(quantity)`

Business interpretation: Volume reflects customer demand. Low volume with high revenue may indicate a high price point that limits sales. High volume with low revenue may indicate over-discounting.

Decision support: Compare volume between promotion period and baseline. If volume did not increase during a promotion, the promotion likely failed to stimulate demand.

## Average Order Value / Panier moyen

Average order value (AOV), also called panier moyen in French, is the average revenue generated per transaction.

Formula: `average_order_value = total_revenue / number_of_transactions`

Business interpretation: AOV reflects how much customers spend per order on average. A high AOV can indicate effective upselling or premium product positioning. A declining AOV may indicate customers shifting toward lower-priced products or smaller order sizes.

Difference with Average Selling Price: Average selling price (ASP) is calculated per unit sold (revenue / quantity), while average order value is calculated per transaction (revenue / number of sales). They differ when customers buy multiple units per transaction.

Decision support: If AOV is low despite high transaction volume, investigate whether product mix or pricing can be optimised to increase revenue per order.

Supported question variants:
- "Qu'est-ce que le panier moyen ?"
- "What is the average order value?"
- "Comment est calculé le panier moyen ?"
- "Quel est le panier moyen du produit 3 ?"
- "What is the AOV?"

## Average Selling Price

Average selling price (ASP) is the average price actually paid per unit, including promotions and discounts.

Formula: `average_selling_price = total_revenue / total_quantity`

Business interpretation: ASP is useful for tracking the real price level in a store or product line. If ASP drops significantly, it may indicate that promotions are too frequent or too deep.

## Promotion Sales Share

Promotion sales share is the percentage of total revenue generated during promotional periods.

Formula: `promo_sales_share = promo_revenue / total_revenue`

Business interpretation: A high promotion sales share (above 40–50%) can indicate that customers only buy during promotions, which erodes margin and creates dependency on discounts.

Decision support: If promo sales share is high and margin is low, investigate whether the promotion calendar can be reduced or rebalanced.

## Promotional Uplift

Promotional uplift measures the incremental performance gain generated by a promotion compared to a baseline period.

Formula: `uplift = (promotion_period_kpi − baseline_kpi) / baseline_kpi`

Business interpretation: Positive uplift means the promotion generated additional sales beyond the baseline. Negative or zero uplift means the promotion did not stimulate demand — it only shifted existing purchases to a lower price.

Decision support: An UNDERPERFORMING_PROMO anomaly is triggered when revenue during a promotion is more than 10% below the baseline. This is a strong signal to investigate the promotion.

## Discount Rate

Discount rate measures the average discount applied relative to the reference price.

Formula: `discount_rate = (reference_price − paid_price) / reference_price`

Business interpretation: A high discount rate reduces margin. An INEFFECTIVE_DISCOUNT anomaly is triggered when a discount of 20% or more does not produce a measurable volume uplift.

Decision support: If discount rate is high but uplift is zero or negative, the promotion is not generating incremental demand. Consider reducing the discount depth or changing the promotion mechanics.

## Price Gap

Price gap measures the difference between a store price and the national reference price.

Formula: `price_gap = store_price − country_reference_price`

Business interpretation: A positive price gap means the store charges more than the national reference. This may be intentional (premium positioning) or an error. A PRICE_ABOVE_REFERENCE anomaly is raised when the store price exceeds the country reference.

Decision support: Investigate price gaps before acting. A store price above reference can be legitimate (local cost structure, premium positioning) or a data error. Check the pricing workflow history before creating a price change request.

## KPIs to use when evaluating a promotion

| Question | KPI to check |
|---|---|
| Did the promotion generate more sales? | Revenue uplift, Volume uplift |
| Was the promotion profitable? | Margin during promo vs baseline |
| Did customers respond? | Volume, Promo sales share |
| Was the discount too deep? | Discount rate, Margin |
| Is the promotion underperforming? | Uplift (< 0 or near 0), UNDERPERFORMING_PROMO anomaly |

## KPIs to use when evaluating a price

| Question | KPI to check |
|---|---|
| Is the price competitive? | Price gap vs country reference |
| Is the price generating revenue? | Revenue, Volume |
| Is the price sustainable? | Margin |
| Is the price correct? | PRICE_ABOVE_REFERENCE anomaly |
